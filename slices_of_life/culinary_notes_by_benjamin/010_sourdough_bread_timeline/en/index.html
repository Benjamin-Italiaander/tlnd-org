🍞 Baking sourdough bread

A simple recipe with a complete timeline

An artisan sourdough bread with a crispy crust, an airy crumb and a wonderful flavour.

This recipe uses the stretch & fold method, so you don’t need to knead the dough for a long time.

Result: 1 loaf of about 750–800 grams after baking.

📋 1. Ingredients

For the bread

IngredientAmount
T65 wheat flour450 grams
Water (25–27 °C)300 grams
Active sourdough starter100 grams
Salt10 grams
Total dough weight860 grams

Hydration: 70%, assuming a starter at 100% hydration.

For activating the starter

IngredientAmount
Existing sourdough starter25 grams
Wheat flour60 grams
Water (25–30 °C)60 grams
Total145 grams

You use 100 grams of this for the bread. You can keep the remaining 45 grams for next time.

Equipment

  • Kitchen scale
  • Large mixing bowl
  • Dough scraper
  • Proofing basket (banneton), or a bowl lined with a tea towel
  • Cast-iron pot with lid
  • Baking paper suitable for high temperatures
  • Sharp blade or knife
  • Clean jar for the starter

🌱 3. Activating the starter

Saturday – 08:00

A strong starter is the foundation of a good sourdough bread.

Method

  • Take a clean glass jar.
  • Add 25 grams of existing starter.
  • Add 60 grams of wheat flour.
  • Add 60 grams of water at 25–30 °C.
  • Mix everything well.
  • Cover the jar loosely.
  • Put the jar in a warm place of about 24–26 °C.

When is the starter ready?

After about 4–8 hours the starter may be ready.

Check that:

  • The volume has at least doubled.
  • Lots of air bubbles are visible.
  • The texture is airy.
  • It smells pleasantly fresh and sour.

Use the starter around the moment it reaches its peak volume.

Has the starter not risen enough by 13:00? Then wait longer and shift the rest of the schedule.

🥣 4. Mixing the dough

Saturday – 13:00

Ingredients

  • 450 grams T65 wheat flour
  • 300 grams water
  • 100 grams active starter
  • 10 grams salt

Method

  • Put 300 grams of water in a large mixing bowl.
  • Add 100 grams of active starter.
  • Stir until the starter has mostly dissolved.
  • Add 450 grams of flour.
  • Add 10 grams of salt.
  • Mix everything until no dry flour is visible.

The dough doesn’t need to be smooth yet.

Resting period

13:10–13:40

  • Cover the bowl.
  • Let the dough rest for 30 minutes.

During this rest the flour absorbs the water and gluten development begins.

🔄 5. Stretch & fold

This technique strengthens the dough without long kneading.

How does a stretch & fold work?

  1. Wet your hands a little.
  2. Grab the edge of the dough.
  3. Gently pull the dough upwards.
  4. Fold it over the middle.
  5. Turn the bowl a quarter turn.
  6. Repeat on all four sides.

That is one full round.

Folding schedule

TimeAction
13:40First stretch & fold
14:10Second stretch & fold
14:40Third stretch & fold
15:10Coil fold

Coil fold

For the last round we use a slightly gentler technique.

  1. Wet both hands.
  2. Slide your hands under the middle of the dough.
  3. Gently lift the dough up.
  4. Let the ends fold underneath themselves.
  5. Turn the bowl 90 degrees.
  6. Repeat the movement.

After the folds, the dough should feel firmer and more elastic.

⏳ 6. First rise – Bulk fermentation

Saturday – 13:00 until about 18:00–20:00

Bulk fermentation starts as soon as the starter is mixed into the dough.

After the last coil fold, leave the dough covered and undisturbed to keep rising.

Ideal conditions

ItemGuideline
Dough temperature24–26 °C
Total first riseAbout 5–7 hours
Target volume increaseAbout 40–60%

How do you know the dough is ready?

  • The dough has grown about 40–60% in volume.
  • Small air bubbles are visible.
  • The dough feels airy.
  • The surface is slightly domed.
  • The dough jiggles a little when you shake the bowl.

Important

The time is only a guideline.

At a room temperature of 20 °C the dough may need considerably longer than at 26 °C.

Only move on to shaping when the dough is sufficiently developed.

A straight-sided, transparent container with volume markings makes this easier to judge.

🥖 7. Shaping the loaf

Saturday – Expected around 18:00–20:00

When the first rise is done, it’s time to shape the loaf.

Method

  • Dust the worktop very lightly with flour.
  • Carefully take the dough out of the bowl.
  • Fold the edges to the middle.
  • Turn the dough over so the smooth side is on top.
  • Gently pull the dough towards you across the worktop.
  • Repeat until the dough has a firm, round shape.

Try to keep as much air in the dough as possible.

Preparing the proofing basket

  • Dust the basket with rice flour or regular flour.
  • Place the dough smooth side down in the basket.
  • Cover the basket well so it doesn’t dry out.

❄️ 8. Second rise – Fridge

Saturday evening to Sunday morning

Put the covered proofing basket in the fridge.

Settings

ItemValue
Fridge temperature3–5 °C
Recommended proofing time12–16 hours
Longer proofPossible, depending on the dough

What happens during the cold proof?

  • Fermentation slows down.
  • Extra flavour develops.
  • The dough becomes firmer.
  • The loaf is easier to score.

Leave the dough in the fridge until you are ready to bake.

🔥 9. Preheating the oven

Sunday – 08:00

  • Put the cast-iron pot with its lid in the oven.
  • Set the oven to 240 °C, top and bottom heat (conventional, no fan).
  • Preheat for at least 45 minutes.

The pot must be completely hot before the dough goes in.

🔪 10. Scoring the loaf

Sunday – 08:45

  • Take the dough out of the fridge.
  • Place a sheet of heat-resistant baking paper on the proofing basket.
  • Carefully turn the basket over.
  • Remove the basket.
  • Dust with a little flour if you like.
  • Use a sharp blade to make a cut about 0.5–1 cm deep.

A long, slightly angled cut gives the loaf room to open up while baking.

🍞 11. Baking the bread

First phase – Lid on

08:50–09:10

  • Carefully take the hot pot out of the oven.
  • Remove the lid.
  • Lower the dough into the pot using the baking paper.
  • Put the lid back on.
  • Put the pot in the oven.

Bake for 20 minutes at 240 °C.

The lid traps the steam, so the bread can rise well.

Second phase – Lid off

09:10–09:30

  • Remove the lid.
  • Lower the oven temperature to 220 °C.
  • Bake for about another 20 minutes.

The bread is done when the crust is a deep golden brown.

With a probe thermometer you can check that the inside has reached about 96–98 °C.

Baking schedule

PhaseTemperatureTime
Preheating240 °C45 minutes
Lid on240 °C20 minutes
Lid off220 °C20 minutes
Total baking time40 minutes

🌬️ 12. Letting the bread cool

Sunday – 09:30–11:00

  • Take the bread out of the cast-iron pot.
  • Place it on a wire rack.
  • Let it cool for at least 1–2 hours.
  • Only slice the bread once the crumb has cooled enough.

Slicing the bread too early can make the inside gummy.

💡 14. Common problems

ProblemPossible causeSolution
Bread stays flatOver-proofed or weak glutenShorter rise or better folding
Bread is denseNot enough fermentationCheck the starter and let it rise longer
Dough is very stickyToo much water for the flourNext time use 10–20 g less water
Bread tears at the sideNot scored enough, or under-proofedScore deeper and check fermentation
Crust is too paleNot baked long enoughBake longer without the lid
Inside is gummySliced too early or underbakedCool longer and check doneness
Bread is very sourLong fermentation or a very sour starterShorter cold proof and use the starter on time

🏆 The most important thing to remember

With sourdough, the dough sets the schedule, not the clock.

The three most important signals are:

  1. The starter: active, airy and roughly doubled.
  2. The first rise: about 40–60% volume increase at 24–26 °C.
  3. The bake: a well-preheated pot and a deep golden-brown crust.

With this foundation you can later adapt the recipe to your own starter, flour and room temperature.
